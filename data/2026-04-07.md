We provide updated estimates on the yield of planet detections that can be expected from the mission under certain assumptions. As of today, large uncertainties remain on the planet occurrence rates, especially for small planets in long-period orbits, and on our ability to detect these planets in the presence of stellar variability and instrumental noise. ### [9] [The Zhamanshin Impact Event: Potential Implications for Environmental Responses and Biological Linkages on Earth and Beyond](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04884)

Main category: astro-ph.EP

TL;DR: 扎曼申撞击坑实际尺寸比先前估计大两倍以上，直径约26.5公里，撞击能量达24万吨TNT当量，可能对0.9Ma前的气候和生物演化产生重大影响

Motivation: 重新评估扎曼申撞击坑的实际尺寸，因为先前估计可能存在严重低估，需要准确量化这一大型撞击事件对中更新世过渡期气候和生物演化的潜在影响

Method: 使用高分辨率地形数据分析扎曼申撞击结构，通过减少尺寸不确定性来确定其真实直径；采用公认的撞击体尺寸、速度、成分和撞击角度范围来估算撞击动能

Result: 扎曼申撞击坑实际直径约26.5公里，为多环结构，比当前最佳估计至少大两倍；撞击动能超过24万吨TNT当量，能量产出比先前估计高7-10倍

Conclusion: 扎曼申撞击事件的规模和能量远超先前认知，这种量级的爆炸性能量事件（类似黄石火山喷发）可能对全球环境产生显著影响，有必要重新评估约0.9Ma前与快速气候转变相关的生物后果

Abstract: At least one large-body (diameter > 1.1 km) hypervelocity cratering event occurred during ~ 0.8-0.90 Ma (Zhamanshin, Kazakhstan) in the Middle Pleistocene Transition period. Analysis designed to reduce uncertainty in the dimensions of the Zhamanshin structure employing high resolution topography demonstrated that it likely generated a ~ 26.5 km diameter multi-ring crater. This is at least two times larger than the current best estimates. Using a range of accepted impactor sizes, velocities, compositions, and angles of impact, such impacts typically yield kinetic energies of impact over 240,000 Megatons (TNT). Explosive energetic events of this magnitude (e.g., Yellowstone Caldera) at other times (K-Pg) have created global environmental effects. The factor of two discrepancy in the dimensions of Zhamanshin increases the kinetic energy yield by factors of 7-10, with significantly larger environmental consequences. This justifies examination of rapid climate transitions linked to biological consequences, including those related to environmental perturbations, at ~0.9 Ma.
